Every character is checked

Each result is decoded again and compared with what went in. Anything the size of a glyph is treated as damage, not noise.

Why the check exists. Compressing a scan hard enough lets the encoder decide two glyphs are the same shape and store one for both — turning a 6 into an 8, with nothing on screen to show it happened. IntactPDF measures for precisely that, and refuses the file rather than let it through.

Why not just use a website?

Online PDF tools are convenient and genuinely good at a lot of things. Three of them they can’t do at all.

A PDF websiteIntactPDF
Your document Uploaded to someone else’s server Never leaves your computer
Size control Low / medium / high — a guess The exact number the form demands
Text safety No check; a changed digit looks identical Measured, and refused if it fails
Limits Size caps, queues, accounts, ads None
Breadth Merge, split, convert, sign, OCR Compression only

The last row is theirs. If you need to merge or convert, use them. If the document is a payslip, a bank statement or an ID scan, uploading it to a free website is the part worth reconsidering.
